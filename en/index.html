Schönberg installation

Arnold Schönberg Center

The ComposerMeet Schönberg installation: at the Center, visitors entered into conversation with the AI avatar of Arnold Schönberg. It opened during the Long Night of Museums.

Developed together with the Arnold Schönberg Center, based on its archive of around 200,000 pages of letters and writings.

All visitors surveyed at the installation would like to see ComposerMeet in other museums too.

What is ComposerMeet?

Your visitors or audience ask a question. On screen, a historical figure answers in real time, with voice and subtitles. In the exhibition, in the concert hall, at a reading or in the classroom.

The answers are based on selected sources: letters, diaries, writings, scholarly analyses and contemporary accounts. An AI system turns them into an answer in the style of the period and cites its sources. The answer takes a few seconds. Meanwhile, everyone can see how the system works.

This turns knowledge into conversation. Nobody just listens, everyone can ask, and the answers stay close to the sources.

Not just music. Goethe on his Italian journey, Marie Curie on research, Kant on the Enlightenment, Bismarck on the founding of the German Empire, Edison on inventing: any well-documented figure can be brought into the conversation.

Fact-based

Every answer is based on documented sources and cites them, so you can check where a statement comes from.

Interactive

Your visitors ask questions, and the avatar responds. It talks with them, not at them.

Contextualised

Life and work are placed in their time, with its social and historical conditions. Where an answer goes beyond the sources, this is flagged.

Find your use case.

Museums

A historical figure who answers questions all day long.

As an installation in an exhibition or foyer. Whether a painter in an art museum, a naturalist in a natural history museum or an inventor in a science museum: visitors talk to them directly.

  • Proven in a museum: a tailor-made installation at the Arnold Schönberg Center Vienna.
  • Designed for continuous operation, runs unattended.
  • Subtitles and a visible process. Accessible and transparent.
  • Minimal hardware, fits into existing spaces.

Concert halls & orchestras

The composer of the evening, live before the concert.

As a conversational concert introduction or moderation of 20 to 30 minutes. The audience asks, Brahms or Clara Schumann answers.

  • Content aligned with your programming.
  • Your institution keeps editorial control.
  • Redundant set-up, ready for the stage.
  • First tested live at the University of Music and Performing Arts Munich (HMTM).

Festivals

A programme item where the audience has its say.

Live on stage, as an installation throughout the festival or hybrid with a livestream. The figure fits your festival theme, be it an anniversary, an era or a question.

  • The audience shapes the conversation.
  • Stage, installation or livestream.
  • Tailored to your festival theme.

Literature houses

The poet doesn’t just read. He answers.

At readings, in exhibitions or at literature festivals, audiences ask writers about their work, their time and their writing. Goethe on Faust, on Weimar, on his travels.

  • Answers from letters, diaries and works, with sources.
  • As a programme item or an installation.
  • Complements readings and talks, does not replace them.

Archives & libraries

A collection that answers back.

Letters, papers and writings become something visitors can question. The figure answers from your collection and cites the reference. This way an archive reaches people who would never set foot in a reading room.

  • Proven with the archive of the Arnold Schönberg Center, around 200,000 pages of documents.
  • From 1,000 to 500,000 pages: also for figures with little surviving material.
  • Every answer cited and verifiable.
  • For exhibitions, anniversaries, teaching and digital humanities projects.

Schools & teaching

History you can talk to.

Students question figures about their work, worldview and life: Kant on the Enlightenment, Curie on research, Goethe on poetry. It is also a starting point for thinking about AI.

  • Working with sources: where does a statement come from?
  • Media literacy: the AI is shown openly and questioned.
  • Adaptable to age, subject and learning goal.
  • Tested at Xplorer Day at the Deutsches Museum Verkehrszentrum.

See ComposerMeet in action.

How an answer is created.

From the spoken question to the avatar’s answer. The audience sees this process live on screen. This makes the system transparent and bridges the short wait.

  1. Question

    Audio input from the audience

  2. Transcription

    The question is turned into text

  3. Source search

    Letters, diaries, writings and analyses are searched

  4. Answer

    Written in the style of the period, with sources

  5. Avatar

    Speaks the answer with voice and facial expression

  • With sources
  • Process visible to the audience
  • Redundant set-up, therefore fail-safe

In operation.

Stable & unattended

Designed for continuous operation. The system has a redundant set-up and compensates for the failure of individual components.

Tailored

Length, tone and format are adapted to your project.

Minimal hardware

A computer, a screen, a microphone and speakers. That’s all it takes, and it fits into existing spaces.

Transparent & accessible

Subtitles, cited sources and a process everyone can see.

GDPR-compliant and copyright-compliant.

Data protection

Our system does not store any conversations. The AI services we use do not train on the input. We conclude a data processing agreement under the GDPR with your institution.

Local version

On request, a local version in which your sources and data never leave your institution.

Copyright

Sources, image and voice are used in compliance with copyright law. The implementation has been legally reviewed.

Not a replica, but an interpretation.

This is about engaging people with knowledge: knowledge that sparks curiosity, and a closer look at history and its sources. We say openly that the answers come from an AI system, and we cite the sources. ComposerMeet does not bring anyone back to life. It invites reflection.

Célest Lang.

Pianist and music communicator with a focus on new concert formats. His work combines artistic practice with technical curiosity and a critical view of historical sources. ComposerMeet emerged at this intersection of music, AI and cultural education and has since grown beyond music.

He developed the prototype for XPLORE, the HMTM Munich competition for new concert formats. Since 2025, ComposerMeet has been funded by Venture Team Kultur in Bavaria.

Career
